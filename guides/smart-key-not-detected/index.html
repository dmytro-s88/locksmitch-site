1. Use the backup start method

Most push-to-start cars can still start with a weak fob. Hold the fob right against the start button and press the button with it, with your foot on the brake. On some cars the fob goes into a marked spot in the console or cup holder instead. Your owner's manual shows the exact place, usually under "key not detected" or "smart key battery".

2. Replace the fob battery

Most fobs use a coin battery, often a CR2032. It costs a few dollars at any pharmacy or hardware store. Open the fob with the small release on the side, note which way the battery faces and swap it. If the fob works afterwards, you're done.

3. Try your spare

If the spare starts the car, the problem is the first fob, not the car. If neither works, the cause is more likely in the car.

4. Rule out interference

Phones, other fobs, metal cases and some chargers can block the signal. Move them away from the fob and try again.

When to call a locksmith

  • The fob got wet, cracked or stopped working after a new battery.
  • You only have one fob and want a spare before it fails completely.
  • The fob works for the doors but the car still says key not detected.
