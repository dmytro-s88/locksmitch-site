1. Replace the fob battery

A weak battery is the most common cause. The range gets shorter first, then the buttons stop working. Most fobs take a coin battery, often a CR2032 or CR2025, sold at any pharmacy or hardware store. Open the case with the small release or a coin in the seam, note which way the battery faces and swap it.

2. Try the spare fob

If the spare works, the problem is in the first fob. If neither fob works, look at the car: a weak car battery or a blown fuse can stop the locks from responding.

3. Let the fob pair again

Some fobs lose their pairing after a battery change. On many cars it comes back after you start the car with that key, or after a few presses of lock and unlock next to the car. Your owner's manual lists the steps for your model. If the fob still doesn't respond, it has to be programmed with a tool.

4. Look at the fob itself

Water, a hard drop and worn buttons are the usual damage. Check for a cracked case, loose battery contacts or a button that no longer clicks.
