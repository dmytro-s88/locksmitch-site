Quick answer

A plain metal key with no chip can be copied at a hardware store or a key kiosk. A chip key, a key with remote buttons or a push-to-start smart key also has to be programmed to the car. Participating Ace Hardware stores and KeyMe kiosks do that for supported cars, AutoZone sells keys but no longer cuts or programs them, and a mobile locksmith does all four key types at your car. In Cumming and North Atlanta, LocksMitch makes a spare chip key from $140, a key with remote from $150 and a smart key from $180. With no working key left, a store or kiosk can't help: that is a locksmith or dealer job.

First: which key do you have?

  • Plain metal key. All metal, no plastic head. Older cars, and door or trunk keys. It only needs cutting.
  • Chip (transponder) key. A blade with a plastic head. Inside is a chip the car must recognize before the engine starts. It needs cutting and programming.
  • Remote head or flip key. Blade and lock, unlock buttons in one piece. Cutting and programming.
  • Smart key. Push-to-start, stays in your pocket, with a small emergency blade hidden inside. Programming, plus cutting the emergency blade.

Not sure? If the key has a plastic head or buttons, treat it as a key with a chip.

Ace's automotive key page says participating stores cut and program car keys, including chip keys, fobs and remotes, for a long list of makes. Why a copy may open the door but not start the car

Cutting copies the shape of the blade. It does not copy the electronics. A key with a chip that was cut but never programmed fits the lock and turns, and the engine still won't start, because the car does not recognize the chip. So "copy" is really two jobs, and the question for any store is whether it does the second one for your car.

Before you go to a store or kiosk

  • Bring a working key. Stores and kiosks copy an existing key. They don't make one from nothing.
  • Know the year, make and model, and whether the car starts with a key or a button.
  • Ask two separate questions: can you cut this key, and can you program it to my car?
  • Ask what happens if it doesn't work in your car: refund, exchange or neither.
  • Test it before you leave the lot. Lock and unlock the door with the blade, start the engine, press every button.

When a mobile locksmith makes more sense

  • You have no working key. Nothing to copy means the key is made from the car: can a locksmith make a key without the original?
  • It is a push-to-start smart key, or the store doesn't have a key for your car.
  • You already bought a key or fob and need it cut and programmed. That is from $99, or $129 for a smart key: key programming. Check the part first: bought a key online?
  • You want it done at home or at work, tested in your car, with a warranty on the key.
  • The car has only one key left. Copying it now costs far less than an all-keys-lost job later.

Common questions

Where can I get a car key copied?

A plain metal key: a hardware store, a key kiosk or a mobile locksmith. A chip key, remote key or smart key: a participating Ace Hardware store or a KeyMe kiosk if your car is supported, a mobile automotive locksmith, or the dealer. The chip has to be programmed to the car, so ask any store whether it programs keys for your year, make and model.

Does AutoZone copy car keys?

No. AutoZone's website says it no longer provides key cutting, in an update dated August 2024, and that it does not offer programming. It sells replacement keys and fobs that a locksmith can cut and program.

Does Walmart copy car keys?

Walmart's website sells replacement fobs but does not describe a car key service of its own. Minute Key lists self-service kiosks inside Walmart stores, and its site shows which locations do car keys and key fobs. Check the location near you before you go.

Does Home Depot copy car keys with a chip?

Home Depot's website sells do-it-yourself key kits for select vehicles, with a pairing tool you use yourself. What a store can cut and whether anyone there programs a chip differs by store, so call and ask for your year, make and model.

Does Ace Hardware copy and program car keys?

Participating Ace stores do. Ace's website says they cut and program chip keys, fobs and remotes, that the key must be bought from Ace, and that you bring your existing key to copy. Check that your local store takes part.

Can a KeyMe kiosk copy a chip key or key fob?

For supported cars, yes. KeyMe says its kiosks copy standard metal car keys, many chip keys, remote head keys and key fobs. Some copies are ready to use and some are shipped within a few days with programming instructions. You need a working key to copy.

Why does my copied key open the door but not start the car?

The blade was cut but the chip was not programmed to the car. The engine starts only with a key the immobilizer recognizes. A locksmith or dealer can program it, if the key is the right type for your car.

How much does it cost to copy a car key?

With LocksMitch in Cumming and North Atlanta: a plain metal key from $75, a chip key from $140, a key with remote or a flip key from $150, a push-to-start smart key from $180, cut and programmed at your car. Can a store copy a car key without the original?

No. Stores and kiosks copy a working key. With no key left, a mobile locksmith makes one from the car itself, or the dealer makes one after the car is towed in.
