Quick answer

It depends on the generation. Older E-series BMWs (about 1995 to 2006) use EWS. Most E-series from about 2002 to 2014 and the first F-series use CAS. F-series from about 2012 to 2019 use FEM or BDC. G-series use BDC2 or BDC3, and the newest models BCP. On EWS, CAS, FEM and BDC cars a new key can be made at the car even when every key is lost. On BDC2 cars we make a spare from a working key, and all keys lost is checked case by case by VIN. On BDC3 and BCP cars a key can only be copied from a working key, onto an original BMW key; with no key left it is a dealer job. BMW and Mini keys start from $350 with LocksMitch.

Why does a BMW key depend on a module?

A BMW key carries a chip, and the car only starts when a module in the car recognizes that chip. That module also stores which keys belong to the car. So a new key is not just cut and paired: it has to be coded to that module, and on some generations the module has to come out of the car to be read. The engine computer (DME), under the hood, holds a matching code called the ISN, which some all-keys-lost jobs need as well.

Where does the module sit?

  • EWS: under the lower dash on the driver side, next to the steering column.
  • CAS: under the steering column, above the pedals. That is where it came out on our 2012 BMW 128i Convertible.
  • FEM and BDC: behind a trim panel in the front footwell.
  • Engine computer (DME): under the hood. Some all-keys-lost jobs need a code read from it.

Before a module comes out, the battery is disconnected. On many BMWs, such as the 1 Series and 3 Series, the battery is in the trunk.

Spare key or all keys lost: what does each system need?

EWS

  • Spare, with a working key: yes.
  • All keys lost: yes. The EWS module is taken out and read.

CAS1 to CAS4+

  • Spare: yes. The working key is used to add the new one.
  • All keys lost: yes. The key data is in the CAS: either the CAS comes out and is read on a bench, or the key is made through the diagnostic port with the ISN read from the engine computer.

FEM and BDC

  • Spare: yes.
  • All keys lost: yes. It needs the ISN from the engine computer, usually read on a bench.

BDC2

  • Spare: yes, from a working key. The BDC is read on a bench.
  • All keys lost: case by case, after a VIN check. The BDC and the engine computer both go to the bench, and it takes longer. Not for hybrids or electric models. Engine computers built after June 2020 (B48 and B58 engines) may need an extra step, so we don't promise those.

BDC3 and BCP

  • Spare: yes, as a copy of a working key onto a new original BMW key.
  • All keys lost: no. Only the dealer can make the first key.

On a BDC2 car with every key lost, call or text first: we check your car by VIN and tell you honestly whether we can do it before anyone drives out.

OBD or bench: what is the difference?

OBD means the programmer is plugged into the car's diagnostic port under the dash, and nothing comes out of the car. A bench job means the module is taken out, opened or connected to the programmer, read, and then put back. It sounds bigger than it is: it is done at your car, and the car works the same way afterwards. It does take longer.

How long does it take?

  • A spare on EWS and CAS cars with a working key: usually about an hour.
  • Bench jobs (FEM and BDC all keys lost, a CAS read on the bench): a couple of hours, two to three on FEM and BDC.
  • BDC2: longer, because the module goes to the bench even for a spare, and for all keys lost the engine computer as well.

We give you the time together with the price. More on timing in general: how long a car key takes.

Original or aftermarket key?

On most BMW and Mini models up to about 2019 you can choose between an original key and a quality aftermarket key that costs less. On BDC3 and BCP cars only a new original BMW key can be added. Either way it is a Comfort Access smart key if your car has Comfort Access, or a regular remote key if it doesn't.

Common questions

How do I know if my BMW has CAS, FEM or BDC?

By the chassis code and the build date. Most E-series from about 2002 to 2014 use CAS, F-series from about 2012 to 2019 use FEM or BDC, and G-series use BDC2 or BDC3. Send the VIN and we look it up for you.

Can a locksmith make a BMW key when every key is lost?

On EWS, CAS, FEM and BDC cars, yes, at the car. On BDC2 cars it is checked case by case by VIN. On BDC3 and BCP cars, about 2020 and newer, only the dealer can make a key when none is left.

Can you program a key for a BMW G-series?

Yes, a spare from a working key. On BDC3 and BCP cars it is a copy onto an original BMW key. With every key lost, BDC2 cars are checked case by case and BDC3 and BCP cars go to the dealer.

What is BDC3?

The body module on most G-series BMWs from about 2020, such as the G20 3 Series and G05 X5. A key can be copied from a working one onto an original BMW key, but there is no aftermarket way to make the first key when every key is lost.

Does the module have to be taken out of my BMW?

Sometimes. CAS spares and some CAS jobs are done through the diagnostic port. EWS, FEM, BDC and BDC2 jobs usually mean the module is taken out, read on a bench and put back, at your car.

How long does a BMW key take?

Usually about an hour for a spare on EWS and CAS cars with a working key, and a couple of hours for bench jobs, two to three on FEM and BDC. BDC2 takes longer. We give you the time with the price.

How much is a BMW key?

From $350 with LocksMitch for BMW and Mini, cut and programmed at the car; all keys lost is quoted by model. A second key in the same visit is 50% off.

Can I use an aftermarket BMW key?

On most BMW and Mini models up to about 2019, yes, and it costs less. On BDC3 and BCP cars only an original BMW key can be added.

Do you make keys for hybrid BMWs?

Spares from a working key are checked by model. All keys lost on BDC2 hybrids and electric models is not possible with our equipment; we tell you before anyone drives out.
