What exactly broke?

The blade snapped, the remote still works

One caller put it this way: the key broke, the fob was still there, but not the metal part. The buttons may still lock and unlock the car, but the engine needs a key that turns and a chip the car recognizes. A new key is cut and programmed so the car starts again.

A piece is stuck in the ignition or a door

Don't push it deeper with another key, a pin or tweezers. It is pulled out with thin extraction tools that hook the cuts on the blade, and the lock is tested after. More: broken key extraction.

The flip key is loose or won't stay open

A flip key can still start the car with a wobbly blade, until the day it doesn't. Get a new key or a spare made while this one still works: a copy costs less than a key made from nothing.

The key turns but the car won't start

If the blade is fine but the engine won't start, the chip in the head may be damaged, for example after a drop or a soaking. The car does not recognize that key anymore, so it is treated like a lost key: a new one is made from the car itself.

The key is burnt or melted

It happens: we made new keys for a family whose keys were lost in a house fire, including a 2024 Chevrolet Silverado 2500HD in Cumming. With no usable key left, the new key is made from the car, at the car.

What should I not do?

  • Don't glue or tape the pieces and try to start the car. The piece can break off inside the ignition.
  • Don't spray oil or glue into the lock.
  • Don't throw the pieces away. The cuts on them help cut the new key, and the head may hold the chip.
  • Don't force a stuck ignition. A key that sticks is often the reason a key breaks.

Can a broken car key be fixed?

A snapped metal blade is not repaired, it is replaced by a newly cut one. Whether the old head, with its chip and remote, can be used again depends on the key and the damage. A cracked head tends to break again, so a new key is often the more reliable fix. Send us a photo of the pieces and we tell you which applies before we come out.

How is the new key made?

  1. The cuts are read from the broken pieces, or from the lock with a decoder when a piece is missing.
  2. A new blade is cut on a machine we carry. We cut car keys ourselves; a hardware store is fine for house keys.
  3. If the car uses a chip, a remote or a smart key, the new key is programmed to the car.
  4. Doors, ignition and buttons are tested before we leave.

Why do car keys break?

A worn key, a sticky ignition, or a heavy key ring hanging from the key and pulling on it every time you drive. If your key is bent, cracked or the cuts look worn, make a fresh spare before it breaks.

Common questions

What should I do if my car key broke?

Keep every piece, don't glue it and don't force it in the ignition. Send a photo of the pieces with the year, make and model, and a locksmith cuts and programs a new key at the car.

Can a broken car key be fixed?

A snapped blade is replaced with a newly cut one, not repaired. Whether the old head with the chip and remote can be used again depends on the damage; a cracked head tends to break again, so a new key is often the better fix.

My key broke in the ignition. What now?

Don't push the piece deeper. A locksmith pulls it out with extraction tools, from $75 with LocksMitch, and checks that the ignition still turns. A new key is priced separately.

Can you make a new key from the broken pieces?

Usually yes. The cuts on the pieces show the depths. If a piece is missing, the key is cut from the lock or from the vehicle code, then programmed to the car if it has a chip.

The remote works but the metal part broke. Will the car start?

Not with the remote alone if the car uses a turn key. A new key is cut and programmed so the engine starts again.

How much does it cost to replace a broken car key?

With LocksMitch: a chip key from $140, a flip or remote key from $150, and from $190 for most makes when no working key is left. Extraction of a stuck piece is from $75.

My key turns but the car won't start. Is the chip broken?

It can be. If the car no longer recognizes the key, a new key is made and programmed from the car itself, like when a key is lost.

Do I need a tow if my only key broke?

No. The new key is cut and programmed where the car is parked. For a car with no working key we come 24/7.

How do I stop a key from breaking?

Keep the key ring light, don't force a sticky ignition, and replace a bent, cracked or worn key with a fresh spare before it snaps.
