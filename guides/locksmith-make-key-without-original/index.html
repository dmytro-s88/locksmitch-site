How is a key made when there is nothing to copy?

A modern car key is two things in one: a metal blade cut to fit the locks, and a chip or smart key the car has to recognize before the engine starts. Copying needs a working key for both. Making a key from scratch gets both from the car instead.

1. The car is identified

Year, make and model, and the VIN if you can read it, tell us which key blank, which chip and which programming your car needs. That is settled on the phone, together with the price, before anyone drives out.

2. The car is opened

If it is locked, we open it with lockout tools, without damage to the paint, glass or weatherstripping.

3. The lock is read

A decoder tool reads the cut depths from a door lock. Usually nothing is drilled or taken apart. Those depths are the key: the same numbers the original was cut to at the factory.

4. A new key is cut

The blade is cut on a machine we carry, to the depths read from the lock, so it is a fresh cut and not a copy of a worn key. Push-to-start cars get the emergency blade cut the same way.

5. The key is programmed to the car

A programmer is connected to the car, usually through the diagnostic port, and the new chip or smart key is registered in the immobilizer. It is the same equipment we use for key programming. Without this step a perfectly cut key opens the door but the engine won't start.

6. Lost keys are erased, everything is tested

The lost keys are removed from the car's memory, so they can no longer start it. This is free with a new key from us. Then we test the doors, the ignition and every remote button before we leave.

This is what we call an all-keys-lost job. The step-by-step plan for the driver is in a separate guide: lost all your car keys?

Can a key be made from the VIN?

The VIN identifies the car and its key system. Automakers keep key codes on file for many cars, and a dealer or a registered locksmith can request one after an ownership check. LocksMitch is NASTF registered, and when every key is lost we make the key from the car itself: the lock is read at the car and the key is programmed to the immobilizer on the spot.

Either way the car has to be there. On any car with a chip, a blade cut from a code alone opens the doors but does not start the engine until the key is programmed to that car. That is why "a key by VIN, mailed to you" rarely solves the problem.

What to have ready

  • The car's details. Year, make, model, whether it starts with a key or a button, and where it is parked. The VIN is on the driver side of the dashboard, visible through the windshield, and on the sticker in the driver's door frame.
  • Proof that the car is yours. A photo ID plus the registration, title or insurance card for the car. We check this before making a key, and a dealer asks for the same. If the papers are locked in the car, say so: we open it first.
  • Any old key or remote you still have, even a broken one or one that no longer starts the car. It can save time.

Common questions

Can a locksmith make a car key without the original?

Yes, for most cars. The cuts are read from the car's own lock with a decoder tool, a new key is cut on the spot, and the key is programmed to the car's immobilizer. No working key is needed, and the car is not towed.

Can a locksmith make a key from the VIN number?

The VIN tells the locksmith which key, chip and programming the car needs, and automakers release key codes by VIN to dealers and registered locksmiths after an ownership check. LocksMitch is NASTF registered and makes the key from the car itself, with the lock read at the car, or from the manufacturer key code when that is the better way.
